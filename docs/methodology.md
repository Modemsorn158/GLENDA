# Protocol and open limitations

## Intended evaluation

Before selecting models, freeze one grouped holdout and use only the development folds for model selection, early stopping and threshold selection. The initial v1 split used the first shuffled five-way `StratifiedGroupKFold` partition as the holdout and a separate five-way partition for development. The one-epoch feasibility results used v1. An audit subsequently found that v1 validation folds contained only 2, 4, 2, 1 and 5 negative-containing groups, respectively. The initial v1 tuning run was interrupted after three completed folds and retained in `outputs/search/tuning/` without selection. V2 preserves every held-out row and connected group but greedily reassigns development groups to balance negative-containing group counts, then negative and positive frame totals. V2 validation folds contain 3, 2, 3, 3 and 3 negative-containing groups and 15–17 positive-containing groups. Reassignment was decided after seeing exploratory development results, although it used group and label counts rather than model scores. V1 and v2 fold scores must not be treated as paired. The seed and source file hashes are recorded. No images are subsampled. Report the resulting distribution rather than describing it as clinical prevalence; a grouped split can differ substantially from the overall frame distribution. Groups are not independent patients where negative case identifiers are unavailable.

After the one-epoch feasibility ablation, the short tuning budget in `configs/tuning.json` compares weighted frozen EfficientNet-B0 at learning rate 0.0003 with weighted partial fine-tuning at 0.0001 for at most three epochs on v2 development folds. These candidates differ in both learning rate and fine-tuning depth, so this comparison cannot isolate the effect of either factor. They were chosen after examining development-only feasibility results, not before the exploratory work; their cross-validation estimates therefore do not represent an unbiased nested evaluation. Select the completed candidate with the highest mean development-fold average precision, breaking exact ties in favor of the first configuration. If any required fold fails, do not select from incomplete results. Choose the decision threshold by pooled out-of-fold F1 only after selecting the candidate, and use the median fold-best epoch count for the final fit. Evaluate the held-out split once after these choices are fixed. The modest budget limits conclusions about convergence.

Out-of-fold scores come from five separately fitted models. Their pooled F1 threshold may not transfer perfectly to the model refit on all development frames, particularly with class-weighted loss. The final fit uses the median epoch count, but each final-fit epoch contains more training frames and optimizer updates than a cross-validation epoch. The sigmoid output is a model score and is not assumed calibrated. The held-out calibration plot and Brier score are assessments only; they must not be used to refit calibration or change the threshold on the same holdout.

Link all frames with the same known case, video, exact SHA-256 digest, or difference-hash screening candidate before splitting. The dHash threshold of three is a reproducible, conservative screening rule, not a diagnosis of duplication; a visual review found some candidate pairs are plainly different. It does not detect all transformed or near-identical images. It can merge independent groups, and unknown patient relationships remain a leakage threat. Seek the dataset owners' case mapping and use stronger duplicate review before claiming patient independence.

The fixed holdout has 22 known linked groups: 21 contain positive frames and three contain negative frames, with overlap between those categories. Its 2,688 negative frames are concentrated in three groups of 2,269, 321 and 98 frames; the largest contributes 84.4% of the negatives. This is a serious constraint on specificity and uncertainty. Do not replace the holdout after examining development results. A genuinely new external cohort with more independent negative cases is needed for stronger generalization claims.

The original study fingerprinted the split manifest and inventory but did not rehash each image before training. Current command-line training and evaluation require a separately trusted inventory digest and verify the relevant image partition before use. This check is a point-in-time check, not protection against subsequent changes; callers of lower-level Python functions must pass or run the verifier themselves. The sequence-bound audit was repeated and reproduced the original inventory hash in the historical environment.

After the completed study, frozen feature blocks were changed to evaluation mode during training, including their stochastic-depth modules. Future searches record current training-source hashes and the inventory digest; threshold selection freezes fold epoch histories, and final fitting rejects missing or mismatched records. The original result remains associated with its original recorded implementation and cannot be reproduced by silently mixing its artifacts with the new code. See [the next-study protocol](next_study.md).

## Historical report

The supplied 15-page report describes a 114-frame test set with 38 positive and 76 negative images, and a 1:2 positive-to-negative ratio. That is not 10% of the 13,811 released frames. Its page 10 settings table labels the ratio as non-pathology to pathology 1:2, which is reversed relative to those reported test counts. Its page 10 results table explicitly compares an earlier image-level internal-validation script and a later video-grouped script on the same 114-image held-out set. This is test reuse, despite prose elsewhere describing a single final evaluation; the comparison alone cannot establish a causal effect of the split change. Its stated scores remain historical claims, not verified outputs of this rebuild. It also acknowledges the altered test prevalence and lack of ROC/PR curves. Its optimizer and learning-rate interpretations should not be treated as causal without controlled comparisons. Relevant pages were visually inspected because Thai text extraction is imperfect. The preserved repository's original `main` commit contains four `src/data` files and no training or evaluation scripts, so the report's reported model-selection and test execution cannot be independently reconstructed from that snapshot. Its `splits.py` groups records into case and video lists but does not itself construct or validate train/test partitions.

## Status

CPU and GPU smoke runs used eight development frames and one epoch; these verify execution only. One-epoch feasibility configurations and a short two-candidate tuning search completed all development folds on the RTX 4050. Their per-fold results and descriptive variation are generated in `reports/`; they are development results, not test results or evidence of convergence. Candidate selection and the out-of-fold threshold were frozen before final fitting and the one-time test evaluation. The selected threshold is near one and fold score distributions differ markedly, as recorded in `reports/oof_score_diagnostics.md`; this creates a substantial risk that it does not transfer to the refitted model. The fixed-threshold holdout results and post-hoc error analysis are recorded in `reports/test_results.md` and `reports/error_analysis.md`. A split manifest alone does not make the evaluation patient-independent or clinically representative. External data are required for an external validation claim.
